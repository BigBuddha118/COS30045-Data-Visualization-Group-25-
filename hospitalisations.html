Road-crash injury hospitalisations

Explore Australian road-crash injury hospitalisations from 2011 to 2021 , based on three hospitalisation workbooks published in 2023. This series measures hospitalised injuries, not police testing or fines.

How have hospitalisations changed nationally?

Annual counts across Australia. Hover over the line for the number reported in each year.

What this shows Annual hospitalisations rose from 34,033 in 2011 to 39,505 in 2021 (about 16% higher).
Read with care These are hospitalisation counts, not risk-adjusted injury rates. Changes may reflect exposure, reporting and other factors.

How do the state and territory trends differ?

Select a state or territory to see its annual hospitalisations. Avoid interpreting larger raw counts as higher individual risk.

Read with care Population sizes differ across jurisdictions. State figures are counts, not per-capita rates.

Which road-user groups are hospitalised?

Select a year to compare hospitalised injuries by road-user group. The groups reflect the published national workbook categories.

Read with care The bars describe hospitalisations, not the risk of injury per road user. Some road-user categories distinguish drivers, passengers and unknown positions.

Hospitalisations for First Nations peoples

Annual hospitalisations reported for First Nations peoples and non-Indigenous Australians. Choose one series or view both; two series use different scales.

Read with care Do not interpret these totals as population-standardised rates or a measure of comparative risk. The workbook also contains records outside the two named status groups, so they do not add to the national total.
